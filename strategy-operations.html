Recommendation

Strategic options

Structure an ambiguous brief into hypotheses, evidence, trade-offs and a recommendation.

Option architecture
1 FrameDefine the decision, boundary and success criteria.
2 HypothesiseIdentify what must be true for each option to work.
3 TestUse quantitative and qualitative evidence to challenge assumptions.
4 Trade offCompare value, cost, timing, capacity, risk and reversibility.
5 RecommendState the preferred path, conditions and consequences.
Option A · Focus

Concentrate resources on the highest-confidence priorities. Faster execution and clearer ownership, but less portfolio breadth.

Option B · Balanced

Protect core priorities while funding a small set of growth bets. Moderate complexity with stronger resilience to uncertainty.

Option C · Accelerate

Increase investment to pursue more opportunities in parallel. Higher upside, with greater dependency and execution risk.

Operating principle

Every forum should end with a clearer decision, owner or next test

Governance adds value when it resolves ambiguity and coordinates action. Measures, papers and meetings should exist because they change a decision or improve execution quality.

Market intelligence

Convert external change into a small number of testable strategic implications.

Signal to action
Market signal

Customer expectations, competitor moves, regulation, technology shifts and cost changes are captured as signals, not conclusions.

Materiality test

Assess scale, confidence, time horizon, affected segment and whether the signal changes current strategic assumptions.

Implication

Translate the signal into a hypothesis about demand, economics, capability, risk or strategic position.

Action

Define whether to monitor, test, accelerate, stop, reallocate or escalate for leadership decision.
