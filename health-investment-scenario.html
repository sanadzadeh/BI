Decision framing

Funding choices should be tested as a portfolio, not assessed one initiative at a time.

The model makes the trade-offs explicit. A service-first scenario can favour capacity and access, a resilience scenario can favour risk reduction, and a constrained-budget scenario can shift funding toward initiatives with stronger quantified value. The result is a comparable decision view rather than a single fixed ranking.

Business-case principle

Financial return is one part of the decision, not the whole decision.

Some investments may have lower directly quantified value but stronger service, resilience or strategic outcomes. A useful model keeps those dimensions visible rather than forcing everything into one financial metric.

AI-assisted modelling workflow

AI is used to accelerate the work around the model, not to replace financial logic, governance or human judgement.

Scenario generation

Generate alternative assumptions and edge cases to test before a recommendation is finalised.

Model review

Use AI to challenge formula logic, identify missing assumptions and surface possible inconsistencies for human checking.

Documentation

Draft model notes, assumptions, business-case narratives and executive summaries from reviewed outputs.

Iteration

Speed up changes to analysis, formulas or prototypes while preserving version control and final review.

Working principle

Use the fastest suitable method, then retain control over the decision.

Some problems need a process change, some need deterministic formulas or automation, and some benefit from AI. The judgement is in choosing the right method, validating the output and keeping the recommendation explainable.
